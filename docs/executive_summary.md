# Executive Summary: Spain Top 50 Song Lifecycle and Playlist Rotation (2024)

**For:** Atlantic Recording Corporation leadership
**Data:** Spain Top 50 daily snapshots, 18 May to 15 October 2024 (historical). These findings do not describe 2026 market behaviour.

## Business problem
Which songs stay on the Spain Top 50, how quickly they rise and fall, and does format or content (single or album track, explicit or clean, duration) relate to how long they last?

## Key findings
1. **The playlist turns over about one song a day.** Mean daily replacement was 2.66% of the playlist (1.33 songs per day). About 30% of entries were songs returning after an absence.
2. **Singles stay on the chart longer than album tracks.** Among songs whose entry was observed, singles appeared a median of 26 days and album tracks a median of 4. This is the most robust result. It holds under alternative attribute rules and after removing the 14 October entry wave, although it weakens there.
3. **Explicit and clean tracks perform the same.** Once censoring is handled, no measure differs significantly after correction. An early apparent difference was an artifact of how songs were observed.
4. **Song length has no relationship with tenure, rank or popularity.**
5. **Most songs debut at their best position.** For observed entries, the median time to peak was zero days: 60% reached their best rank on their first observed day.
6. **Popularity rises over the first month on the chart** (median +10.5 points for songs with repeated readings) and is associated with better rank. These are associations, not causes.

## Watch-out: the 14 October 2024 wave
20 songs first appeared on 14 October, 19 of them by Bad Bunny. This drove the highest turnover of the period. It affects the album comparison and the stage results. The data do not show why it happened, so we recommend checking release records before using these results to plan releases.

## Playlist rotation patterns
- Turnover was 1.0–1.5 songs per day from June to September, and rose to 2.7 in October because of the 14 October wave.
- 64 of 149 days (43%) had no new entries, so rotation comes in bursts as well as a steady flow.

## Lifecycle behaviour
- 138 of 188 songs left during the window. 50 were still on the chart at its end.
- 35 songs returned after a gap, so tracking re-entry matters for accurate tenure figures.
- Among songs on the chart at the end of the window, 20 were new entries, 13 were in decline, 15 were mature (stable outside the top 10), and 2 were in the top 10 or climbing.

## Recommendations
1. **Plan for a fast turnover.** The median first run on the chart was 14 days, and songs that left did so after a median span of 9.5 days. Campaigns should be built around the first two weeks.
2. **Measure debut performance separately.** Many songs debut at their peak, so launch metrics matter more than later growth.
3. **Investigate the 14 October wave.** Confirm release dates for the 20 songs before concluding anything about album strategy.
4. **Prefer singles where chart longevity is the goal,** pending confirmation with release-date data, since the album effect may partly reflect how album tracks enter.
5. **Do not base format decisions on explicit status or song length.** The data show no reliable link.
6. **Improve data collection:** full snapshots for every day (15 October is incomplete), release dates, and a stable song identifier.

## Important limitations
- **Historical only.** About five months of 2024 data from one playlist.
- **Censoring.** Songs already charted on 18 May, and songs still charted on 14 October, have unknown start or end points. Tenure figures are for songs whose entry and exit were both observed, or are lower bounds.
- **Incomplete snapshot.** 15 October 2024 holds 8 of 50 songs and is excluded, so its absences are not counted as exits.
- **Associations, not causes.** Format, release timing and the 14 October wave are closely linked.
- **Identity.** 14 song titles credited to different artists are kept separate and need review.
